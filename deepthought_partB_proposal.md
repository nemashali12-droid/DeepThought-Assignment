## Question 1: How Else Would You Find Companies Matching the Federer Profile?

### Method 1: DSIR (Department of Scientific and Industrial Research) Recognition Register
**What it is:** DSIR maintains an official register of companies with government-recognized in-house R&D facilities. Available on the DSIR website as a downloadable list.

**Why it works for this ICP:** DSIR recognition is one of the explicit C3 differentiators in the Federer scoring rubric. Every company on this list has demonstrable in-house R&D, which eliminates the single hardest-to-verify criterion remotely. It also signals government engagement — companies on this list are likely promoter-driven and technically led.

**Limitations:** The list is not always current. Companies get recognized but the website may lag. Requires cross-referencing with MCA data for revenue and sector verification.

---

### Method 2: Association of Diagnostics Manufacturers of India (ADMI) + AIMED (Association of Indian Medical Devices)
**What they are:** ADMI covers IVD manufacturers. AIMED covers all medical device manufacturers. Both publish membership directories and participate in public procurement tender processes.

**Why they work:** These are not generic directories — they are trade body memberships that require active manufacturing. The ADMI list specifically is a near-complete map of genuine IVD kit manufacturers in India.

**Limitations:** ADMI is IVD-only. AIMED is broader but less curated for specialty. Neither provides revenue or founder data — cross-referencing with MCA is still needed.

---

### Method 3: PLI Scheme Approved Company Lists (Ministry of Commerce / Invest India)
**What they are:** The Indian government publishes the list of companies approved under PLI schemes for Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices, and Food Processing. These are available on the Invest India website and respective ministry portals.

**Why they work for this ICP:** PLI approvals are granted to companies with verified manufacturing capacity, investment commitments, and projected export revenue. A company on the PLI pharma list is almost definitionally a manufacturer — and the PLI medical devices list surfaces specialty IVD and surgical instrument makers that are hard to find via generic search.

**Limitations:** PLI lists favor larger companies (the investment thresholds screen out very small manufacturers). They over-represent generic pharma for the pharma PLI. Most useful for medical devices and food processing PLI.

---

### Method 4: BIRAC (Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council) Grantees + BIG Scheme Recipients
**What they are:** BIRAC is the DBT's industry arm that funds biotech startups and mid-size companies through the Biotechnology Ignition Grant (BIG), SBIRI, and other schemes. Lists of grantees are published in BIRAC's annual reports and are searchable on their website.

**Why they work:** BIRAC grantees are by definition biotech manufacturers or researchers making physical products. The BIG scheme has funded companies like OmniBRx (Ahmedabad), Lazuline Biotech (Hyderabad), and dozens of specialty biotech companies across India that would never appear on a general business directory. This is probably the single best source for finding early-to-mid stage specialty biotech companies that fit the Federer profile.

**Limitations:** Only covers companies that applied for and received government grants. Profitable, bootstrap-funded companies may be absent. Grantee information has 1–2 year lag.

---

### Method 5: CHEMTECH World Expo + India International Science Festival (IISF) Exhibitor Lists
**What they are:** CHEMTECH World Expo is India's largest chemical and bioprocess technology exhibition (Mumbai; biennial). IISF is the government's science exhibition. Both have exhibitor directories.

**Why they work:** Companies that pay to exhibit at CHEMTECH are typically manufacturers looking to sell to other manufacturers or attract partners. The exhibitor list is therefore a curated list of genuine manufacturers — and since the event is technical, it skews toward companies with real R&D depth. OmniBRx exhibited at CHEMTECH 2024 — exactly the kind of company we're looking for.

**Limitations:** Exhibitor lists require manual processing. Small companies may exhibit without a strong web presence, making secondary verification harder.

---

### Method 6: GIDC Industrial Estate Directories (Gujarat-specific)
**What they are:** Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation maintains directories of industrial units across their 200+ estates (Naroda, Vatva, Changodar, Sanand, Savli, etc.).

**Why they work:** For Ahmedabad, Vadodara, and Surat specifically, GIDC directories are a near-complete listing of manufacturing companies by estate. Filtering by NIC code for pharma (NIC 21), chemicals (NIC 20), and medical devices (NIC 26/32) surfaces manufacturers that don't have strong digital presence.

**Limitations:** GIDC directories are Gujarat-specific. Do not apply for other states. Require cross-referencing with MCA for revenue and ownership.

---

### Method 7: Indian Patent Office (IPO) Patent Database + WIPO PatentScope
**What they are:** The Indian Patent Office's public database and WIPO's PatentScope both allow searching by applicant country (India) and technology class. IPC codes for biotechnology (A61K, C12N, etc.) and medical devices (A61B) can be searched to surface companies with genuine IP.

**Why they work:** A company with filed patents is almost certainly a specialty manufacturer — commodity producers don't file patents. This method specifically surfaces C3-strong companies that might be too small to appear in commercial databases.

**Limitations:** Patent filing ≠ active manufacturing. Need to cross-check that the company is operationally active and making the patented product.

---

### Method 8: Pharma Export Promotion Council (Pharmexcil) and APEDA Export Data
**What they are:** Pharmexcil maintains a directory of pharmaceutical exporters. APEDA (Agricultural & Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) covers agri-biotech exporters. Both are available on their respective websites.

**Why they work:** Export activity is one of the C6 growth signal thresholds. A company actively exporting pharma or biotech products to regulated markets (EU, US, Africa) is almost certainly a genuine specialty manufacturer with some quality credibility.

**Limitations:** These cover a broad range — generic pharma dominates Pharmexcil. Requires filtering by product type to isolate specialty.

---

### Method 9: LinkedIn Boolean Search (Company-Level)
**What it is:** Using LinkedIn's People and Company search with filters for:
- Industry: Biotechnology / Pharmaceuticals
- Location: Ahmedabad (or other target cities)
- Company size: 11–500 employees
- Then searching for founders/MDs at those companies and checking credentials

**Why it works:** LinkedIn is the best single source for founder background verification — the hardest part of the C4 criterion. IIT/IISc/NIT alumni are searchable by education. PhD-level founders often list their publications. This method directly solves the C4 gap that leaves many companies as Borderline.

**Limitations:** Many Indian SME founders do not maintain active LinkedIn profiles. Ahmedabad in particular has lower LinkedIn penetration than Bengaluru or Hyderabad. Works better for mid-size companies than micro-enterprises.

---

### Method 10: Clinicaltrials.gov + CDSCO Drug Approval Database (for pharma companies)
**What they are:** Clinicaltrials.gov lists Indian companies sponsoring or participating in clinical trials. CDSCO (Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation) maintains a list of approved drug manufacturing licenses in India.

**Why they work:** A company running clinical trials is not a trader — it's developing products. CDSCO license data specifically identifies manufacturing license holders by state and product category.

**Limitations:** CDSCO data is raw and unstructured. Clinical trials skews toward larger companies with R&D budgets.

## Question 2: The 1000-Company Proposal

### Objective
Build a list of 1,000 genuinely ICP-qualified specialty manufacturer companies across India within one month. Quality standard: each company must be a verified specialty manufacturer (not trader/service), India-based, with at least one C3 differentiator and one C6 growth signal documented.

---

### Where to Source the Initial Universe (~3,000–4,000 candidates to get 1,000 passes)

1. **DSIR Recognition Register** — ~2,500 companies (full India list); download as CSV; filter to manufacturing NIC codes
2. **BIRAC Annual Reports (2019–2024)** — ~500 additional biotech grantees; scrape from PDFs
3. **PLI scheme approved lists** — ~300 medical device + pharma manufacturers; available on Invest India website
4. **ADMI + AIMED membership directories** — ~400 IVD and medical device manufacturers
5. **GIDC estate directories** (Gujarat) — ~500 chemical/pharma/biotech companies in GIDC estates
6. **Pharmexcil exporter directory** — ~800 pharma exporters (will need heavy filtering for specialty vs generic)
7. **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — pull companies in target sectors and cities with 10–500 employees
8. **IndiaMART / TradeIndia category scraping** — "specialty chemical manufacturer", "diagnostic kit manufacturer", etc.; yes this surfaces some traders but it also surfaces genuine manufacturers that don't appear in official databases

**Expected initial universe: 4,000–6,000 raw company names**

---

### How to Automate the Qualification Step

**Phase 1: Data Enrichment (Week 1–2)**
Use Claude + web scraping to enrich each company record with:
- Website URL (from company name + city + Google search)
- Revenue estimate (Tracxn or Tofler API if available; otherwise scrape the MCA filings)
- Company size (employee count from LinkedIn or Tracxn)
- Auto-disqualify flags (is it a CRO? is it a generic pharma company? is it a subsidiary?)

Tools: Python + Playwright for web scraping; Claude API for classification; Tofler/Tracxn for structured financial data.

**Phase 2: Auto-Disqualify Filtering (Week 2)**
Build a classifier that flags companies for removal based on:
- Revenue > Rs.500Cr (from Tracxn/Tofler data)
- Known subsidiary indicators (parent company name in MCA data; "Limited" within a known conglomerate)
- Service company keywords in product description ("CRO", "contract research", "testing laboratory", "clinical trial")
- Generic pharma keywords ("generic formulation", "bulk drug", "branded generic")
- No website flag

**Expected yield after auto-disqualify: 2,000–2,500 remaining companies**

**Phase 3: Federer Score Semi-Automation (Week 2–3)**
For each remaining company:
- Feed website text + LinkedIn founder page text to Claude API with a structured prompt to score C1–C6
- Flag ambiguous companies for human review
- Auto-confirm companies where 4+ criteria score Strong with evidence

The Claude prompt would look like:
```
You are evaluating whether [Company Name] is a specialty manufacturer matching this ICP profile. 
Here is their website text: [TEXT]
Here is their founder's LinkedIn bio: [TEXT]
Score each criterion as Weak/Moderate/Strong with one line of evidence:
C1: Are they a manufacturer (not trader/service)?
C2: Are they India-based (HQ or primary manufacturing)?
C3: Are they differentiated (patents, DSIR, USFDA/EU-GMP, proprietary products)?
C4: Does the founder/MD have a technical/scientific background?
C5: Are they in a growing sector with tailwinds?
C6: Do they show at least 2 active growth signals?
Return JSON format: {C1: {score, evidence}, C2: ...}
```

**Expected yield after scoring: ~1,500 companies with Federer scores**

**Phase 4: Human Quality Control (Week 3–4)**
Filter to B-band and A-band companies (score ≥ 60). Expected: ~1,000–1,200 companies.
Human reviewers check:
- All A-band companies (score 80+): spot-check 20% for accuracy
- All C-band borderline cases (score 40–59): manual review of each to promote or reject
- Random 10% sample of B-band companies to validate scoring accuracy

### Weekly Plan

**Week 1 — Source and Compile**
- Download DSIR, PLI, ADMI, BIRAC, GIDC lists
- Scrape LinkedIn for company names in target segments and cities
- Compile into a master deduplicated spreadsheet (~4,000 company names with basic info)
- Build web enrichment pipeline (website URL, employee count, sector keyword)
- Target: 4,000 companies enriched with website URLs and basic sector classification

**Week 2 — Enrich and Filter**
- Run auto-disqualify filtering on all 4,000 companies
- Enrich with revenue data (Tracxn/Tofler; scraping MCA where needed)
- Remove: generic pharma; service companies; subsidiaries; revenue > Rs.500Cr; no website
- Target: 2,000–2,500 companies through initial filter

**Week 3 — Score with AI**
- Feed each company through Claude API scoring prompt (C1–C6)
- Run founder background extraction from LinkedIn
- Flag companies scoring ≥ 60 as provisional passes
- Target: ~1,200–1,500 scored companies; ~1,000–1,200 provisional passes

**Week 4 — Human QC and Finalize**
- Human review of all A-band (80+) companies: spot-check facts; verify revenue is current
- Human review of all borderline (40–59) companies: promote or reject
- Fill personalization hooks for top 500 companies (A and B-band)
- Verify all auto-disqualify flags are accurate (especially subsidiaries and revenue near Rs.500Cr)
- Target: 1,000 confirmed ICP-qualified companies with scores, evidence, and personalization hooks

### Quality Control Approach

**False Positive Prevention (companies that look like ICP but aren't):**
1. Always verify "manufacturer" claim with evidence (plant address, ISO 13485, production process description) — never accept self-description alone
2. Revenue verification: always check MCA filing date; if last filing is >18 months old, flag for manual research (company may have grown past Rs.500Cr)
3. Subsidiary check: search parent company name in MCA; search for "Group" relationships; check if promoter owns >51%
4. CRO/service trap: search for "CRO", "clinical trials", "testing laboratory", "contract research" in company description; if found, manual review required

**Missing Data Handling:**
- Founder background (C4) is the hardest criterion to auto-score. Default score: Moderate (10) if company has DSIR recognition or regulatory approvals (implies technical management). Score Weak (0) if no signals. Never assume Strong without positive evidence.
- Revenue unknown: do not auto-disqualify but flag for manual research; score C1–C5 normally; note revenue unknown in the record

**Borderline Cases:**
- Any company scoring 40–59: requires human review before inclusion
- Any company near revenue limit (stated Rs.300Cr+): manual MCA current-year revenue check
- Any company in grey-area segments (e.g., CRO + manufacturing hybrid): manual review

### What This Proposal Does NOT Promise

1. 1,000 companies does not mean 1,000 outreach-ready leads. An estimated 20–30% will need a phone call or direct verification to fill in missing founder background before being outreach-ready.
2. Revenue data will lag by 1–2 years for private companies. Companies growing fast (PLI recipients, defence electronics, genomics tools) may have crossed Rs.500Cr since their last public filing. The quality control process flags these but cannot eliminate the gap.
3. Companies found through scraping vs. directories will have uneven data quality. Scraping-sourced companies will typically need more manual verification than DSIR or PLI list companies.
